## Grounded Findings: Real-World Deployment Outcomes

### 1. Scaling Behavior: Synthetic Data Has Diminishing Returns

Microsoft Research's **SynthLLM** framework documents a modified scaling-law behavior for synthetic data:

- Performance plateaus at approximately **300 billion tokens** — beyond this point, adding more synthetic data yields only minor improvements.
- **Larger models are more data-efficient**: an 8-billion-parameter model reaches near-optimal performance with ~1 trillion synthetic tokens, while a smaller 3-billion-parameter model requires ~4 trillion tokens to reach comparable performance.

This suggests that practitioners cannot simply scale synthetic data volume indefinitely and expect commensurate gains — a meaningful constraint on the "more is better" assumption often applied to real-world data pipelines.

> *Source: Microsoft Research SynthLLM blog post. Note: the underlying paper details and independent replications of these specific token counts were not retrieved; these figures should be treated as practitioner reports pending peer-reviewed confirmation.*

### 2. Benefit: Enabling Training When Real Positive Examples Are Rare

**ING Bank** deployed synthetic data to solve a class-imbalance problem in an IT incident prediction use case:

- Natural software failure rate in their IT change pipeline: **2.5%** — too low to support direct supervised training.
- Solution: synthetically generated failure examples were used to augment the training set, enabling a viable prediction model.
- This approach has since been **scaled to 20+ business applications** at ING.

This is a concrete benefit case: synthetic data enabled a production ML system that would otherwise have been infeasible due to data scarcity, without requiring any relaxation of privacy constraints.

> *Source: Datacebo case study (vendor-published). Independent verification not available.*

---

### 3. Benefit: Inference Cost Reduction via LLM Distillation

A **knowledge distillation** pipeline — fine-tuning a smaller model (GPT-4o-mini) on outputs generated by a larger model (GPT-4.1) — was reported to yield a **70% reduction in AI inference costs** at a volume of 10,000 calls/day for a high-volume voice AI platform.

This use case illustrates synthetic data's role in **model compression and cost optimization**: the larger model's outputs serve as synthetic training signal for the smaller, cheaper model.

> *Source: Prodinit consulting case study (vendor's own publication, no independent verification). The distillation pipeline details are sparse. This finding should be treated with particular caution.*
